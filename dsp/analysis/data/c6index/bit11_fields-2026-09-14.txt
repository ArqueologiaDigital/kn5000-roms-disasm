  5  THE RELATION, AND ITS NULL:  does `mid != 0' imply `addr8 == 0'?

     AT WORD LEVEL:   105 of 105 words with mid != 0 carry addr8 == 0
     ⚠ inflated -- 99 of those 105 words are ONE shape (`C63').

     AT SHAPE LEVEL (the honest granularity):
       cells with mid != 0 : 4   (1,0x21) (4,0x62) (4,0x63) (7,0x22)
       of those, all-zero-addr8 : 4

       NULL, stated as a rate and not after the fact: 6 of 11 cells in the whole
       family are all-zero-addr8, a base rate of 0.545.  If `mid' had nothing to do
       with it, 4 independent cells land that way with p = 0.545^4 = 0.0885.

       MEASURED 4 of 4.  ★ SUGGESTIVE, NOT PROOF at p = 0.0885 -- four cells cannot carry more than this, and
       the honest reading is: `mid != 0' and a payload have never been seen
       together, over 4 shapes and 105 occurrences in BOTH products.  To promote
       it, find a `mid != 0' shape WITH a payload (refutes) or a fifth without.

     ⇒ what this DOES settle: the decomposition extends.  The space is sparse,
       `mid' takes 4 values, `sel' 10, and the two fields are not independent.
       What it does NOT settle: what `mid' and `sel' MEAN -- which is the one
       question the whole 169 turns on (sect. 202).

     ⚠ `(ST)' marks hi12 bit 4, the STORE.  A register-load word that ALSO stores is
       excluded from `is_ldptr()' by an explicit guard -- `dsp_disasm.py:493' says so
       ("A member carrying the bit-4 store is excluded: its second effect has an
       unproven target off mode 2").  Those words are blocked on the STORE TARGET,
       which is a different and already-documented problem from the encoding itself.
