4. How this review was conducted

The review was carried out by twelve independent agents in four stages, none of which could modify anything:

  1. Measure — three parallel factual audits: the rate of progress over time, the decode coverage with denominators, and a census of the project's failure modes.
  2. Assess — four independent lenses over that shared evidence. Two of them were deliberately opposed: one required to build the strongest possible case that the effort is stagnating and the strategy is wrong, the other the strongest case that this is a healthy plateau preceding a breakthrough. The remaining two examined complementary approaches never tried, and the machinery of the work itself.
  3. Challenge — each assessment handed to a separate agent instructed to refute it at file-and-line level, and to flag where an assessment was too weak.
  4. Synthesize — one answer, required to drop anything the challenge refuted and to say what it dropped.

Both opposed lenses carried the same constraint: if the evidence does not support your case, say so plainly — a lens that cannot return "the case is weak" is not a lens. All figures below were re-derived by the review directly against the repositories, not taken from the project's own summaries.

5. Rate of progress

5.1 What moved

Two always-on measurements changed for the first time in the project's history, both within the last 36 hours. The saturation rate in the zero-input bucket fell from 5.303 % to 4.924 % of 186,394,560 conversions — a difference of exactly 706,040, which is precisely one conversion per silent frame. And a memory cell moved from a stuck full-scale value to the chorus oscillator's designed +114-per-frame ramp: the modulation reaching its intended destination for the first time.

5.2 What did not move

The end-to-end reading is unchanged: every one of 826,040 quiet frames and 313,960 loud frames produces a peak output of zero. That has held from section 77 through section 226.

More importantly — and this is the review's central measurement — the decode itself has not advanced:

5.3 Where the machine actually is

The review decomposed the saturation census itself and found something the project had not stated. Every clipping site in the shipped build is in the fixed 60-word preamble that runs before any effect, on every frame of every preset — six instructions, each saturating on 100 % of the 706,040 frames where the input is exactly zero. Their counts sum to 9,178,520; adding one further site gives 9,178,556, which is the entire quiet-bucket total, exactly.

Real audio does enter the chip — an input audit records 2,880,002 samples with a peak of 21,796 out of 32,768. But before that audio reaches any effect at all, the machine's own fixed preamble computes a constant 1.720 times full scale from boot-fixed coefficients and saturates. Everything downstream is zero.

6. Stagnation, or a plateau?

The review declined to answer this as a single question, and split it into three parts with different verdicts. That split is the most useful thing in the report.

6.1 The behavioural work: a genuine converging plateau

The chain of the last eight sections is a real bisection, in which each step consumed the output of the previous one: the input cell is the send → forcing it open activates the first effect body → the output block is exonerated by provenance rather than by liveness → the first non-zero output is an artefact of the test rig → the rail is in the shipped build, not the rig → the site is one instruction at 1.720 × full scale → the suspected multiply is faithful → the coefficient base is correct. The current blocker is a single named, untested reading with five pre-registered falsifiers.

Verdict: a plateau one decision from moving, not stagnation.

6.2 The decode work: genuinely stagnant, and measurably so

38.5 % has not moved in four days. What the decode effort produced in that time was closures — fields declared undecidable, candidate readings refuted. These are real results that lower the ceiling, but they do not raise the score.

The review's phrasing is worth quoting exactly: "a project that only produces them is not decoding, and nothing in the read path flags the difference." The project's own documentation does not distinguish between a section that decoded something and a section that closed a route, so the distinction was invisible to the loop making the decisions.

6.3 A third thing, neither stagnant nor progressing: the bookkeeping is degrading

The review found the project's own records drifting out of agreement with each other:

  • The count of shipped fixes reads five, seven, "7 of 7", eight and nine across five different documents. There is no enumerated list of them anywhere.
  • The index of standing rules stops at rule 18, while rules 19, 20 and 21 are cited by number in at least eight files. Rule 20 has no definition anywhere — it is only ever invoked.
  • A standing-constraints block still quotes the pre-fix saturation rate after the fix shipped.

The most serious instance. A cross-model comparison against a sibling instrument — proposed independently during this very review as the highest-value unopened channel — was already run on 22 July, with a negative verdict: zero shared coefficients, delay lengths falsified, an intersection of one value across 962 pairs. That result exists in a notes directory the project's index does not sweep, and appears nowhere in its analysis tree. The review counts this as the eleventh occurrence of exactly the failure the index was built to prevent — and diagnoses the cause precisely: the index is repo-scoped while the evidence is not.

6.4 The verdict

The method is sound and the current tactical target is correct. The strategy is wrong in its allocation. Three cheap decode experiments are available and unrun; one fix that is proven by construction has sat unshipped for two days; and the hardware channel has never been dispatched in nine days.

7. Prospects for complete decoding

"100 %" turns out to mean three different things here, with very different answers.

TargetDefinitionTodayReachable?
D1 Every corpus word executable without guessing 38.5 % No. The ceiling from this ROM alone is about 93.3 %; 205 words carry a formally closed field.
D2 Every field of every word read 21.8 % No. One two-bit field alone appears in 1280 of 3057 words (41.9 %). One of its three observed values is read by a shipped gate; the rest are not.
D3 Every word graded as proven, measured, or arbitrary-within-a-known-class, with no plain guess load-bearing in the audio path — Yes, and largely by relabelling rather than new discovery.

The structure of what remains is also unhelpful for anyone hoping for a single breakthrough. The review ran the project's own leverage analysis: 86 unexecutable slots depend on 40 distinct unknowns, and no single unknown blocks more than seven slots. The best available bundle unlocks 25 slots for three unknowns. There is no one big win left; it is a bundle problem.

7.1 The finding that changes the picture — and its correction

The review reported that a field covering 41.9 % of the corpus had been closed on a technicality. It argued the closure rested on no single program image containing both halves of a minimal pair — while the machine loads two images at once — and proposed selecting one effect into the first unit and a reverb preset into the second to bring a pair into a single frame.

A follow-up pass, commissioned to pre-compute the experiment before building it, found the proposal not viable and the diagnosis wrong — in a way that produces a better result.

Why the proposed experiment fails. The two instructions sit at 83.3 % and 5.3 % through their respective program ladders, twenty cursor advances deep against three, a factor of 12.1 apart in executed instructions, on different accumulators with different coefficient banks. A difference measured there could not be attributed to the field under test. Worse, one half of the pair reads exactly zero in both input conditions across nine archived experiments — it is dead code, three slots downstream of a known failure.

Why the closure is nonetheless wrong, for a different reason. The tool that searched for minimal pairs counted one-bit transitions across a two-bit field, so one of the three transitions was invisible to it. Counting properly yields 17 pairs, three of them co-resident within a single image — the best being two adjacent instructions in the kernel header that runs on every frame of every preset. All three observed values of the field have been present together in every run this project has ever made. No effect selection is required, and none ever was.

The real blocker is not co-residency but the absence of a hypothesis. Every instrument the project owns already prints both kernel sites. What is missing is a candidate meaning to test them against — and the one perfect pair is a chain, in which the second instruction's input is the first one's result, so the two cannot be made to read the same coefficient.

The honest restatement is therefore "no discriminator", not "no co-resident pair". Those are different claims and only the first is true. The productive route is to extend the partial reading that already exists for one of the field's values, not to run a cross-unit comparison.

A second, genuinely cheap instance of the original shape does survive: another undecoded bit has both halves of a pair inside a single image, with a ready-made selection recipe. One program load would decide whether an instruction the disassembler prints 62 times as a no-operation actually is one.

7.2 The honest answer

Complete decoding in the strictest sense is not achievable from this ROM and should be formally abandoned as a goal. The achievable target is the third definition plus roughly 45–50 % on the first — and, in the review's words, the difference between "we stalled at 38.5 %" and "we reached the honest ceiling" is currently three unrun experiments and a relabelling pass.
