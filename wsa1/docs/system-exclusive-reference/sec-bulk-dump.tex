\chapter{Bulk dump}\label{ch:bulk}

Bulk dump transfers the instrument's internal data as System Exclusive. The instrument is
silent for the duration, and the transfer uses the MIDI~1 sockets only.

\section{Categories and what each transmits}
The \textsc{sysex bulk dump} screen offers five categories. Each sends one or more
\textsc{btr} messages at fixed addresses. The menu's names and the published address map's
names for the same data differ; both are given.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lllllr}
\toprule
category & area & \textsc{adr} & \textsc{siz} & length & blocks \\
\midrule
\textsc{system, part \& midi} & \textsc{panel} & \bytes{40 00 00} & \bytes{00 00 20} & 32 & \\
                              & & \bytes{40 00 20} & \bytes{00 12 60} & 2\,400 & \\
\addlinespace
\textsc{sound}                & \textsc{sound memory} & \bytes{20 00 00} & \bytes{10 00 00} & 262\,144 & 32 \\
\addlinespace
\textsc{combination}          & \textsc{combination} & \bytes{50 00 00} & \bytes{00 06 00} & 768 & \\
                              & & \bytes{50 06 00} & \bytes{05 40 00} & 90\,112 & 16 \\
\addlinespace
\textsc{sequencer}            & \textsc{sequencer} & \bytes{60 00 00} & \bytes{00 18 00} & 3\,072 & \\
                              & & \bytes{60 18 00} & \bytes{01 70 00} & 30\,720 & \\
                              & & \bytes{62 08 00} & \multicolumn{2}{l}{set at send time} & \\
\addlinespace
\textsc{total keyboard}       & \multicolumn{5}{l}{the four categories in the order above} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Lengths are in source bytes; on the wire each occupies twice as many, because of the
payload encoding described below.

\begin{note}{These lengths are confirmed by the manufacturer}
Every length in the table was decoded from the program, and Technics prints the same set
in its \emph{Reference Guide}. The two agree area by area, including \textsc{sequencer},
whose last block the guide also calls variable rather than giving it a size.
\end{note}

The \emph{blocks} column is internal structure, not extra messages: \textsc{sound} sends
one header covering all 262\,144 bytes, which is 32 repeats of an 8\,192-byte layout, and
\textsc{combination}'s second block one header covering 90\,112 bytes as 16 repeats of
5\,632. Those are the instrument's 32 sounds and 16 combinations.

\textsc{total keyboard} runs the four categories one after another in the order listed,
each closed by its own end-of-category message, with a single end-of-dump message after
the last.

\begin{note}{The transmit order is not the menu order}
The table above is in transmit order, which a capture from a real instrument confirms:
\textsc{system, part \& midi} goes first. The \textsc{sysex bulk dump} menu lists the
same categories in a different order, with \textsc{sound} at the top. A receiver should
take each transfer's \textsc{adr} as the statement of what is arriving, and not infer the
category from its position in the session.
\end{note}

The \textsc{sequencer} category is the only one whose final message has no fixed length ---
it carries as much recorded material as the instrument holds.

\begin{note}{The address is an exclusive address, not a memory address}
\textsc{adr} is a position in the published MIDI exclusive address map, which
chapter~\ref{ch:params} gives in full. It is not an address inside the instrument:
\textsc{sound} and \textsc{combination} are held side by side in the machine, yet their
exclusive addresses are 786\,432 apart.

For a bulk dump it is best treated as a fixed name. On reception the instrument compares
\textsc{adr} and \textsc{siz} against a fixed list and performs no arithmetic on them, so
seven of the eight blocks are pinned on all six bytes; see below.
\end{note}

\section{Data message --- BTR}
\begin{center}
\bytes{F0 50 2D 04 \textit{nn} 11}\quad
\bytes{\textit{a2 a1 a0}}\quad
\bytes{\textit{l2 l1 l0}}\quad
\bytes{\textit{data\ldots}}\quad
\bytes{\textit{c}}\quad
\bytes{\textit{sum}}\quad
\bytes{F7}
\end{center}

\begin{description}
\item[\bytes{\textit{nn}}] \textsc{md}, the model: \bytes{00} or \bytes{01}.
\item[\bytes{\textit{a2 a1 a0}}] \textsc{adr}, the destination, three bytes, most
  significant first, seven bits each: the value is $a_2 \times 128^2 + a_1 \times 128 + a_0$.
\item[\bytes{\textit{l2 l1 l0}}] \textsc{siz}, the length in \emph{source} bytes, encoded
  the same way.
\item[\bytes{\textit{data}}] \textsc{dt}, the payload, encoded as described below.
\item[\bytes{\textit{c}}] \textsc{cn}, the continue identifier: \bytes{01} if further
  messages follow, \bytes{00} on the last.
\item[\bytes{\textit{sum}}] \textsc{sm}, the checksum.
\end{description}

\subsection*{How much each frame carries}
A complete message may not exceed 256 bytes including \bytes{F0} and \bytes{F7}
(chapter~\ref{ch:framing}), and the two frame shapes reach that limit differently, because
a \textsc{btr} header is eleven bytes where a \textsc{cdd} continuation header is two:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
frame & total & payload bytes & source bytes \\
\midrule
\textsc{btr}, the first of a transfer & 255 & 240 & 120 \\
\textsc{cdd}, every one after it      & 256 & 250 & 125 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Longer transfers are split across as many \textsc{cdd} frames as needed. Those figures are
the observed maxima in a dump from a real instrument, not a stated limit; a sender may use
less.

\section{Payload encoding}
Each source byte is sent as two message bytes, \textbf{high nibble first}:
\[ b \;\longrightarrow\; (b \gg 4),\quad (b \mathbin{\&} \mathtt{0F}) \]
So source byte \bytes{F1} appears on the wire as \bytes{0F 01}, and the payload occupies
twice as many bytes as the length field states. A receiver rejoins them as
$((\text{first} \mathbin{\&} \mathtt{0F}) \ll 4) \mathbin{|} (\text{second} \mathbin{\&}
\mathtt{0F})$.

An odd number of payload bytes is invalid.

\section{Checksum}
Let $S$ be the eight-bit sum of every byte from the identifier \bytes{50} through the
continuation flag inclusive. Then
\[ \mathit{sum} \;=\; (0 - S) \mathbin{\&} \mathtt{7F} \]
The leading \bytes{F0}, the checksum byte itself and the trailing \bytes{F7} take no part
in the sum. Equivalently, the sum of every byte after \bytes{F0} including the checksum is
zero modulo 128.

\begin{caution}{The checksum is not verified for every family}
On reception the checksum is checked only for families \bytes{7E}, \bytes{2B},
\bytes{2C} and \bytes{2D}. For the other accepted families a wrong checksum passes
unremarked.
\end{caution}

\section{Requesting a dump --- DRQ}
A dump can be asked for over MIDI; the front-panel \textsc{send} button is not the only
way to start one. The request names a category by the top byte of \textsc{adr}:

\begin{center}
\bytes{F0 50 2B 04 \textit{nn} 11 \textit{area} 00 00 \textit{x x x} F7}
\end{center}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
\textsc{adr} & category requested \\
\midrule
\bytes{40} & \textsc{system, part \& midi} \\
\bytes{20} & \textsc{sound} \\
\bytes{50} & \textsc{combination} \\
\bytes{60} & \textsc{sequencer} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The three bytes of \textsc{siz} are ignored; any values are accepted. The
instrument then runs exactly the transfer the corresponding menu row would have run.

There is no request for \textsc{total keyboard}: the four categories must be asked for one
at a time.

\begin{caution}{A dump request is screen-gated; a parameter request is not}
Both are \textsc{drq}, family \bytes{2B}, and they do not behave alike. The routine that
takes a request naming one of the four categories above tests the panel before anything
else: it proceeds on the \textsc{sysex bulk dump} screen, or when two further panel bytes
both read \bytes{01}, and otherwise sets status \bytes{11}.

\textbf{That gate is not the whole story, and this edition cannot finish it.} Against the
emulated instrument a category request was refused with \bytes{F0 50 29 7E F7} on the
\textsc{sysex bulk dump} screen as well as at rest, and no request ever started a
transfer. Something downstream of the panel test refuses as well, and what it is has not
been isolated. Read the section above as the message the instrument parses, not as a
transfer anyone has seen start this way.

A request naming a \emph{parameter} (chapter~\ref{ch:params}) has no panel gate at all
--- nothing in the parameter engine reads the panel --- and it does answer, at rest, both
when the address is good and when it is not. That is the one \bytes{2B} whose reply can be
relied on.
\end{caution}

\section{Opening a transfer}
A dump begins with a two-step exchange. Each step is attempted up to three times.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The sender transmits \bytes{F0 50 21 04 \textit{md} 11 F7} and expects
  \bytes{F0 50 22 04 \textit{nn} 11 F7}.
\item The sender transmits \bytes{F0 50 22 04 \textit{md} 11 F7} and expects
  \bytes{F0 50 23 7E F7}.
\end{enumerate}

\textsc{md} is the sender's own: \bytes{00} from a keyboard and \bytes{01} from a rack,
substituted on the way out as chapter~\ref{ch:messageset} describes. A capture from a real
rack carries \bytes{04 01 11} in both steps.

Read from the instrument's side, each step is gated twice: the panel must be on the
\textsc{sysex bulk dump} screen, and the handshake state must be the one that step
expects --- zero for \bytes{21}, one for \bytes{22}. A \bytes{21} that arrives on any
other screen, or a \bytes{22} before its \bytes{21}, is answered with nothing at all.
The state advances only on a step that passed both gates, so a sender that opens a
transfer twice without closing the first will find its second \bytes{21} ignored.

If the exchange succeeds, every subsequent data message must be acknowledged. If all three
attempts of \emph{either} step fail, \textbf{the dump proceeds anyway, without
acknowledgements}: both exits fall through to the same place with the acknowledgement bit
still clear, and the transfer runs regardless.

The unacknowledged dump is paced by a fixed wait of the order of 50\,ms between messages.
The figure is given loosely on purpose: measured off the wire the gaps have a median of
45\,ms, but the host timestamps jitter by as much as the quantity being measured, so only
the order of magnitude survives. The 2.05\,s the opening exchange waits per attempt is
large enough to measure cleanly, and was: three attempts, 2.04 and 2.00\,s apart.

\section{Acknowledgement and retry}
With acknowledgements in force, each data message must be answered with
\bytes{F0 50 23 7E F7}. Any other reply causes the same message to be retransmitted, up to
three attempts, after which the transfer is abandoned.

A reply is awaited for about 5.1 seconds; during the opening exchange the wait is about
2.05 seconds.

\section{Continuation --- CDD}
Data messages after the first open with \bytes{F0 50 7E}, \textsc{cdd}, followed by
\textsc{dt}, \textsc{cn}, \textsc{sm} and \bytes{F7}. A continuation carries no address.

\section{Closing}
At the end of each category the sender transmits \bytes{F0 50 27 7E F7} and waits for an
acknowledgement. At the end of the whole transfer it transmits \bytes{F0 50 28 7E F7}. If
the transfer was abandoned after three failed retries it transmits \bytes{F0 50 29 7E F7}.

\section{Receiving}
When the SX-WSA1R is the receiver it answers each data message with
\bytes{F0 50 23 7E F7} to accept, \bytes{F0 50 24 7E F7} to reject, and
\bytes{F0 50 2A 7E F7} when the incoming data is larger than the destination.

While a bulk-dump session is open the instrument accepts only the \bytes{50} identifier;
universal messages are not honoured until the session ends.

\begin{caution}{The instrument must be on the SYSEX BULK DUMP screen to receive}
A dump cannot be pushed to an idle instrument. Reception happens only while that screen is
displayed; a dump sent at any other time is parsed, discarded, and not reported.

A dump offered on the MIDI~2 input is never received either, at any time. This is what lies
behind the published instruction to use MIDI~1 for bulk transfers.
\end{caution}

Seven of the eight patterns are pinned on all six bytes, address and length together.
Sending a correct address with a shortened length is not a short transfer --- it is an
unrecognised message, refused with \textsc{error 41}. Only \textsc{sequencer} block~3
has its length read as a number, because that block's size varies. An arbitrary
destination cannot be written.

The one length the message itself controls is \textsc{sequencer} part~3, which is capped at
330\,752 bytes; beyond that, and whenever the destination fills mid-message, the
instrument answers \bytes{F0 50 2A 7E F7} and reports \textsc{error 21}.

\begin{caution}{Memory-full is treated as end-of-dump}
When the SX-WSA1R is \emph{sending} and the receiver answers \bytes{F0 50 2A 7E F7} to
report that its memory is full, the instrument treats that exactly as it treats
\bytes{F0 50 28 7E F7}, end of dump. It stops, and it reports success. A sender that needs
to know whether the data arrived cannot rely on the instrument to notice.
\end{caution}
