\chapter{Universal System Exclusive}

\section{Recognised messages}
The instrument honours two universal messages, and transmits both.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
message & meaning \\
\midrule
\bytes{F0 7E 7F 09 01 F7} & General MIDI System On \\
\bytes{F0 7E 7F 09 02 F7} & General MIDI System Off \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Turning General MIDI on restricts the sounds and operations available and may rearrange
the percussion map. General MIDI System Off has no effect if General MIDI is already off.

Both messages are also written into Standard MIDI Files produced by the instrument, as a
five-byte System Exclusive event at delta time zero.

\section{Messages not recognised}
A controller expecting any of the following will receive no response:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
message & \\
\midrule
\bytes{F0 7F 7F 04 01 \textit{ll} \textit{mm} F7} & Master Volume \\
\bytes{F0 7E \textit{dd} 06 01 F7} & Identity Request \\
\bytes{F0 7E 7F 09 03 F7} & General MIDI 2 System On \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The instrument does not implement Identity Reply, so it cannot be auto-detected by a
controller that relies on it. The entire Universal Real Time identifier \bytes{7F} is
unrecognised.

\section{Practical consequences}
\textbf{A General MIDI message sent during a bulk dump breaks the dump.} While a transfer
is open the instrument accepts only messages beginning \bytes{F0 50}. One beginning
\bytes{F0 7E} is treated as a reception failure: the transfer is abandoned and
\textsc{error 41} is reported. General MIDI mode is left unchanged.

\textbf{The instrument announces its own changes.} Entering or leaving General MIDI mode
makes it transmit the matching message, for a change made at the front panel as well as
one made by loading a song. Neither direction is affected by the \textsc{exclusive}
filter.

\begin{caution}{The announcement stops for good once you send one}
The interlock that stops the instrument echoing a General MIDI message back is armed by
the first such message it receives after power-on, and is never disarmed. Once a
controller has sent either message, the instrument stops announcing its own General MIDI
changes for the rest of the session. A controller that tracks the instrument's mode by
listening for these messages will silently stop being told.
\end{caution}

\textbf{General MIDI state travels in a dump.} It is carried in the second
\textsc{system, part \& midi} block, so restoring that category restores the General MIDI
setting with it. A dump taken in General MIDI mode is not the same file as one taken out
of it.

\section{The identifier is checked once}
Once \bytes{50} or \bytes{7E} has been accepted the instrument does not look at it again;
the third byte alone selects the message. \bytes{F0 50 7F 09 01 F7} is therefore acted on
as General MIDI System On despite carrying the Matsushita identifier, and a Technics
message sent with \bytes{7E} in the identifier position works equally well. Send the
conventional identifier, but expect a receiver to be lenient.

Nothing else about General MIDI mode changes the System Exclusive implementation: every
family is accepted exactly as before, with the same encoding, checksum, handshake and
error reporting.
