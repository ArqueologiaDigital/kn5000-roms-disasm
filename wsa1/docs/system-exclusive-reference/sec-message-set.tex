\chapter{The Technics message set}\label{ch:messageset}

\section{Fields}
Technics' published specification names the fields of a Technics System Exclusive message.
This reference uses those names.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
field & value & meaning \\
\midrule
\textsc{sox}  & \bytes{F0} & exclusive status \\
\textsc{idc}  & \bytes{50} & Technics identifier \\
\textsc{cmd}  & & command, see below \\
\textsc{pc}   & \bytes{04} & product category, \textsc{wsa} \\
              & \bytes{7E} & \textsc{dmy}, the dummy used where no product is named \\
\textsc{md}   & \bytes{00} & model, SX-WSA1 \\
              & \bytes{01} & model, SX-WSA1R \\
\textsc{ver}  & \bytes{11} & exclusive version 2.1 \\
\multicolumn{3}{l}{\emph{then, for the messages that carry data:}} \\
\textsc{adr}  & 3 bytes & a 21-bit address, seven bits each, most significant first \\
\textsc{siz}  & 3 bytes & length in source bytes, encoded the same way \\
\textsc{dt}   & & the data \\
\textsc{cn}   & \bytes{00}/\bytes{01} & continue identifier \\
\textsc{sm}   & & checksum \\
\textsc{eox}  & \bytes{F7} & end of exclusive \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{note}{VER is a version, not part of a model identifier}
\bytes{11} is the version of the \emph{exclusive specification}, 2.1. It is not an
operating system version --- this instrument's operating system 2.0 sends
\textsc{ver}~=~\bytes{11} --- and it is not part of a model code. The model is
\textsc{md}, the byte before it.
\end{note}

\section{Commands}
After \bytes{F0 50}, \textsc{cmd} selects the message.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textsc{cmd} & & \\
\midrule
\bytes{21} & \textsc{hrq} & hand shake request \\
\bytes{22} & \textsc{hrt} & hand shake routine \\
\bytes{23} & \textsc{ack} & acknowledge \\
\bytes{24} & \textsc{nak} & negative acknowledge \\
\bytes{25} & \textsc{tmp} & tempo data \\
\bytes{27} & \textsc{eok} & end of block \\
\bytes{28} & \textsc{end} & end \\
\bytes{29} & \textsc{err} & error \\
\bytes{2A} & \textsc{ful} & memory full \\
\bytes{2B} & \textsc{drq} & data request \\
\bytes{2C} & \textsc{itr} & individual data \\
\bytes{2D} & \textsc{btr} & data block \\
\bytes{7E} & \textsc{cdd} & continuing data \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The instrument accepts one further value that the published table does not list,
\bytes{7F}, and does not accept \bytes{26}.

\section{Which fields each message carries}
\textsc{ack}, \textsc{nak}, \textsc{eok}, \textsc{end}, \textsc{err} and \textsc{ful} name
no product: they carry \textsc{pc}~=~\bytes{7E} \textsc{dmy} and stop there, with no
\textsc{md} and no \textsc{ver}. That is why they are five bytes long.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
message & \\
\midrule
\bytes{F0 50 23 7E F7} & \textsc{ack} \\
\bytes{F0 50 24 7E F7} & \textsc{nak} \\
\bytes{F0 50 27 7E F7} & \textsc{eok}, end of block \\
\bytes{F0 50 28 7E F7} & \textsc{end}, end of dump \\
\bytes{F0 50 29 7E F7} & \textsc{err} \\
\bytes{F0 50 2A 7E F7} & \textsc{ful}, memory full \\
\bytes{F0 50 21 04 \textit{md} 11 F7} & \textsc{hrq}, hand shake request \\
\bytes{F0 50 22 04 \textit{md} 11 F7} & \textsc{hrt}, hand shake routine \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textsc{drq}, \textsc{itr} and \textsc{btr} carry \textsc{pc}, \textsc{md} and
\textsc{ver}, then \textsc{adr} and \textsc{siz}, then \textsc{cn} and \textsc{sm}.
\textsc{itr} and \textsc{btr} carry \textsc{dt} between them; \textsc{drq}, which asks
rather than tells, carries none. \textsc{cdd} carries \textsc{dt}, \textsc{cn} and
\textsc{sm} with no address at all.

\begin{caution}{MD is rewritten on transmission}
The keyboard transmits \textsc{md}~=~\bytes{00}, the rack \bytes{01}. The rack substitutes
the byte as the message leaves, on every \bytes{21}, \bytes{22}, \bytes{2C} and \bytes{2D}
message it sends, and recomputes the checksum afterwards, so the substitution is invisible
to a receiver that validates it. A bulk dump captured from a real rack carries
\bytes{04 01 11} throughout.

Reception is model-blind. Both values are accepted by both models, on every command that
carries \textsc{md}, and reach the same handler; a message is never refused, ignored or
treated differently because of it. A receiver must therefore accept both, and a file
dumped from one model loads into the other.
\end{caution}

A message whose \textsc{pc}, \textsc{md} or \textsc{ver} the instrument does not recognise
is rejected with \textsc{error 40}.

\section{Tempo}
\textsc{tmp} carries a tempo and nothing else:

\begin{center}
\bytes{F0 50 25 \textit{lo} \textit{hi} F7}
\end{center}

Six bytes. Unlike every other Technics command it carries \textbf{no \textsc{pc}, no
\textsc{md}, no \textsc{ver} and no checksum}, and any bytes sent beyond the fifth are
ignored. The tempo is nine bits sent low nibble first:
$\mathit{value} = \mathit{lo} + 16\,\mathit{hi}$. The accepted range is 40 to 300 beats per
minute and a value outside it is treated as 120.

This message is the one thing the \textsc{exclusive} filter governs; see
chapter~\ref{ch:settings}. It is also one of the two things the rack does not support; see
chapter~\ref{ch:models}.

\section{Parameter commands}
\textsc{drq} requests and \textsc{itr} writes an individual parameter. Both address the
space chapter~\ref{ch:params} sets out; chapter~\ref{ch:examples} works one through byte by
byte.
