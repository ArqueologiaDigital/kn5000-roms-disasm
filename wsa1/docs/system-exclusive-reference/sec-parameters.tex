\chapter{Parameter messages}\label{ch:params}

Two commands address individual parameters rather than transferring blocks.
\textsc{itr} (\bytes{2C}) writes one; \textsc{drq} (\bytes{2B}) requests one.

\begin{center}
\bytes{F0 50 2B 04 \textit{md} 11 \textit{a2 a1 a0} \textit{s2 s1 s0} \textit{cn} \textit{sm} F7}
\end{center}

\section{The address}
\textsc{adr} is a single 21-bit address, sent as three seven-bit bytes, most significant
first. It is the same address space the bulk dump uses --- a dump and a parameter write are
two ways of reaching one map. The published map divides it as follows.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
\toprule
\textsc{adr} & address & area & subarea & \\
\midrule
\bytes{00 00 00} & \bytes{000000} & \textsc{system} & & real time \\
\bytes{00 08 00} & \bytes{000400} & \textsc{system} & & non-real time \\
\bytes{00 10 00} & \bytes{000800} & \textsc{part} & \textsc{common} & real time \\
\bytes{00 18 00} & \bytes{000C00} & \textsc{part} & \textsc{common} & non-real time \\
\bytes{00 20 00} & \bytes{001000} & \textsc{part} & \textsc{individual} & part 1, real time \\
\bytes{00 20 40} & \bytes{001040} & \textsc{part} & \textsc{individual} & part 1, non-real time \\
\bytes{00 3F 40} & \bytes{001FC0} & \textsc{part} & \textsc{individual} & part 32, non-real time \\
\bytes{00 60 00} & \bytes{003000} & \textsc{part} & \textsc{special} & real time \\
\bytes{00 68 00} & \bytes{003400} & \textsc{part} & \textsc{special} & non-real time \\
\bytes{10 00 00} & \bytes{040000} & \textsc{normal sound} & \textsc{individual} & real time \\
\bytes{18 00 00} & \bytes{060000} & \textsc{drum sound} & \textsc{individual} & real time \\

The last five are the bulk-dump categories of chapter~\ref{ch:bulk}. The ones above them
are what an individual parameter message reaches.

\section{Parts}
A part occupies \bytes{80} bytes of the map, split in half: the first \bytes{40} are its
real-time parameters and the second \bytes{40} its non-real-time ones. So for part $n$,
counting from~1, the middle byte of \textsc{adr} is $\mathtt{20} + n - 1$, and the last
byte is the parameter, below \bytes{40} for a real-time parameter and \bytes{40} or above
for a non-real-time one.

All 32 parts take the same 57 parameters.

\section{What the instrument actually accepts}
Not every address in the map is a parameter. In the \bytes{00} area the instrument accepts
108 distinct addresses, which the tables below enumerate in full.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lllr}
\toprule
\textsc{adr} middle & area & subarea & parameters \\
\midrule
\bytes{00}, \bytes{01} & \textsc{system} & & 22 \\
\bytes{08} & \textsc{system} & non-real time & 4 \\
\bytes{10}, \bytes{11} & \textsc{part} & \textsc{common} & 24 \\
\bytes{20}--\bytes{3F} & \textsc{part} & \textsc{individual} & 57 each \\
\bytes{60} & \textsc{part} & \textsc{special} & 1 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Under \bytes{11}, values of the last byte from \bytes{03} upward are admitted in three
runs, one per effect processor:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
run & processor & \\
\midrule
\bytes{20}--\bytes{36} & \textsc{effect1} & type, twenty values, send, dynamic control \\
\bytes{40}--\bytes{56} & \textsc{effect2} & type, twenty values, send, dynamic control \\
\bytes{60}--\bytes{75} & \textsc{reverb}  & type, twenty values, dynamic control \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Within a run the first address is the effect type and the next twenty are its parameters,
whose meaning depends on the type selected. Any value of the last byte outside the three
runs is accepted by the parser and then discarded, producing no error and no effect.

\section{Parameters and the bulk dump are the same bytes}\label{sec:paramdump}

The \textsc{part} and \textsc{system} parameters and the \textsc{panel} bulk dump --- the
category the menu calls \textsc{system, part \& midi} --- are two views of one object. That
dump carries \bytes{100000} to \bytes{100980}, and every parameter of the \bytes{00} area
is one byte, or a field of one byte, inside it.

This matters in both directions. An editor that writes parameters and a librarian that
restores dumps are competing for the same storage, so a dump restored after a parameter
write discards that write. It also gives a way to confirm any row of the tables below
without opening the instrument: dump the category, change the one parameter, dump again,
and the two dumps differ at the stated address and nowhere else.

\subsection*{Where a part's record sits}
Each part occupies a \bytes{40}-byte record in that dump, and the offsets in
table~\ref{tbl:partparams} are relative to the start of it. The records are in two runs:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
part & record begins at \\
\midrule
1 to 8 & \bytes{1000A2} + \bytes{40} $\times$ (part $-$ 1) \\
9 to 32 & \bytes{1002E2} + \bytes{40} $\times$ (part $-$ 9) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{caution}{The part records are not one array}
The obvious formula, \bytes{1000A2} + \bytes{40} $\times$ (part $-$ 1), is correct for the
first eight parts and wrong for the other twenty-four. A \bytes{40}-byte gap after part~8
holds common-block storage, and the parts resume above it. Code that assumes a single
stride will read and write the wrong part for parts~9 upward, and will corrupt two common
blocks on the way past.
\end{caution}

\section{What a parameter accepts}

Each parameter has a fixed data length and a range of accepted values. A value outside the
range is rejected without an error message. The \emph{bits} column gives which bits of the
dump byte belong to the parameter; where that is not all of them, other parameters share
the byte, and a dump editor changing one must preserve the rest.

Three parameters accept an enumerated set rather than a range; sending any other value has
no effect.

\begin{caution}{Two parameters where the published range and the range check disagree}
The \emph{values} column below is the range the instrument's own check enforces. For all
but two parameters that is also the published range. The exceptions:

\begin{description}
\item[\bytes{00 00 08} \textsc{master tuning}.] Published as \bytes{C0}--\bytes{00}--\bytes{3F},
  a signed value with \bytes{00} at concert pitch, spanning 427.3 to 453.0\,Hz. The range
  check admits 48 to 192. A dump from a real machine holds \bytes{00} there --- the
  published centre, and not a member of the checked range.
\item[\bytes{00 \textit{nn} 05} \textsc{effect2 on/off}.] Published as \bytes{00}--\bytes{01}.
  The range check admits 0 to 127, and the instrument passes the value through a
  substitution before storing it, so reading it back does not return what was written.
\end{description}

Send what the published specification gives.
\end{caution}

\begin{note}{The accepted values are what a write is checked against}
They are what the instrument takes from an \textsc{itr}, not a guarantee about what a
stored byte contains. A librarian should not validate a dump by checking it against these
ranges.
\end{note}

\section{Names}
Every name in the tables is the one Technics' own parameter tables print for that address.
None is inferred. Two of them are corroborated a second way, from a dump taken off a real
machine: every part's \textsc{panpot} reads 64, the value the instrument displays as
\textsc{ctr}, and the 32 parts' \textsc{basic channel} bytes read an unbroken ramp from 0
to 31.

The \bytes{08} block is marked receivable but not requestable: the instrument accepts a
write to it and will not report its contents. Its four addresses are commands rather than
settings --- they initialise a section, switch play mode, or ask for a store.

\section{Whole-category request}
\textsc{drq} also requests an entire bulk-dump category, as described in
chapter~\ref{ch:bulk}. There \textsc{adr} names one of the lower five rows of the map
above.

\section{The sound areas}
\textsc{adr} values beginning \bytes{10} and \bytes{18} reach \textsc{normal sound} and
\textsc{drum sound}, the individual sound being edited rather than any stored one. This
edition does not enumerate their parameters.
