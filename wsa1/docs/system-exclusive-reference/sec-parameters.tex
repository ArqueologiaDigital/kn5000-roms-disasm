\chapter{Parameter messages}\label{ch:params}

Two message families address individual parameters rather than transferring blocks.
Family \bytes{2C} writes; family \bytes{2B} requests. Both share one address space.

\begin{center}
\bytes{F0 50 2B 04 \textit{nn} 11 \textit{r} \textit{s} \textit{p} \ldots F7}
\end{center}

The three bytes after the model triple select what is addressed.

\section{Parameter area}
When \bytes{\textit{r}} is \bytes{00}, \bytes{\textit{s}} selects a block and
\bytes{\textit{p}} selects a parameter within it.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llr}
\toprule
\bytes{\textit{s}} & block & parameters \\
\midrule
\bytes{00} & common block 0 & 16 \\
\bytes{01} & common block 1 & 6 \\
\bytes{08} & write only & 4 \\
\bytes{10} & common block 10 & 20 \\
\bytes{11} & common block 11 & 4, plus a further 68 values \\
\bytes{20}--\bytes{3F} & part 0 to part 31 & 57 each \\
\bytes{60} & single parameter & 1 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The part blocks are regular: \bytes{\textit{s}} is \bytes{20} plus the part number, and all
32 parts take the same 57 parameters. A few parameters carry more than one data byte.

Under \bytes{\textit{s}} = \bytes{11}, values of \bytes{\textit{p}} from \bytes{03} upward
are admitted in three runs --- \bytes{20}--\bytes{36}, \bytes{40}--\bytes{56} and
\bytes{60}--\bytes{75}. Any other value in that block is accepted by the parser and then
discarded, producing no error and no effect.

Counting the part block once, the area holds 108 distinct parameters.

\section{Parameters and the bulk dump are the same bytes}\label{sec:paramdump}

The individual parameters and the \textsc{system, part \& midi} bulk dump are two views of
one object. That dump carries addresses \bytes{100000} to \bytes{100980}, and every
parameter in the \bytes{00} area is one byte --- or a field of one byte --- inside it.

This matters in both directions. An editor that writes parameters and a librarian that
restores dumps are competing for the same storage, so a dump restored after a parameter
write discards that write. It also gives a way to confirm any row of the tables below
without opening the instrument: dump the category, change the one parameter, dump again,
and the two dumps differ at the stated address and nowhere else.

\subsection*{Where a part's record sits}
Each part occupies a \bytes{40}-byte record, and the offsets in
table~\ref{tbl:partparams} are relative to the start of that record. The records are in
two runs:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
part & record begins at \\
\midrule
0 to 7 & \bytes{1000A2} + \bytes{40} $\times$ part \\
8 to 31 & \bytes{1002E2} + \bytes{40} $\times$ (part $-$ 8) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{caution}{The part records are not one array}
The obvious formula, \bytes{1000A2} + \bytes{40} $\times$ part, is correct for the first
eight parts and wrong for the other twenty-four. A \bytes{40}-byte gap after part 7 holds
common-block storage, and the parts resume above it. Code that assumes a single stride
will read and write the wrong part for parts 8 upward, and will corrupt two common blocks
on the way past.
\end{caution}

\section{What a parameter accepts}

Each parameter has a fixed data length and a range of accepted values. A value outside the
range is rejected without an error message. Where the tables give a range of
\bytes{0}~or~\bytes{1} the parameter is a single bit, and several such parameters usually
share one byte of the dump --- so a dump editor changing one of them must preserve the
others in the same byte.

Three parameters accept an enumerated set rather than a range; sending any other value has
no effect.

\begin{caution}{A dump may hold values a parameter message would refuse}
The accepted values in the tables are what the instrument takes from a \bytes{2C} write.
They are not a guarantee about what a stored byte contains. In a dump taken from a real
machine, one field --- common block \bytes{00}, parameter \bytes{08} --- holds
\bytes{00} although a write of \bytes{00} to it would be rejected.

So a librarian should not validate a dump by checking it against these ranges, and should
not assume a value read out of a dump can be written back one parameter at a time.
\end{caution}

\section{Names}
The name column is filled only where the instrument itself or its published manual names
the byte. A name marked \dag{} is a match on value range or bit count rather than a
statement by the machine. Blank means this edition does not know what the parameter is;
its address, length and accepted values are still correct, and are enough to read it out
of the instrument and watch what changes.

One parameter does not store what it is sent: \bytes{20 05} accepts \bytes{0}--\bytes{127}
and passes the value through a substitution before storing it, so reading it back does not
return what was written.

\section{Whole-area request}
Family \bytes{2B} also requests an entire category, as described in chapter~4. Here
\bytes{\textit{r}} takes the region byte rather than \bytes{00}.

\section{A third region}
Both families accept \bytes{\textit{r}} values of \bytes{10}, \bytes{18} and \bytes{19},
which carry an address and a count. What they address is not covered by this edition.
