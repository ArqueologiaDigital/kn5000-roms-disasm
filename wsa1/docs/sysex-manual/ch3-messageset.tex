\chapter{The Matsushita message set}

\section{The third byte}
\estab\ After \bytes{F0 50}, the third byte selects a message family. It is matched
against a 15-record table at \texttt{prom\_b} \bytes{0xF5115B}, of which 14 are live and
the fifteenth is an \bytes{0xFF} terminator. The accepted values are

\begin{center}
\bytes{21 22 23 24 25 27 28 29 2A 2B 2C 2D 7E 7F}
\end{center}

\estab\ \bytes{0x26} is \emph{not} accepted. The WSA1's own product identifier is
\bytes{0x2D}: every outgoing bulk-dump header in \texttt{prom\_b} begins
\bytes{F0 50 2D 04 00 11}.

\section{The model triple}
\estab\ The families \bytes{0x21}, \bytes{0x22}, \bytes{0x2B}, \bytes{0x2C} and
\bytes{0x2D} all continue with three bytes: \bytes{0x04}, then \emph{either} \bytes{0x00}
\emph{or} \bytes{0x01}, then \bytes{0x11}. Both middle values are separate literal records
in the grammar --- they are not read from a setting, a strap or NVRAM at match time.

\estab\ The enquiry handler copies the responding unit's three bytes into RAM
\bytes{0x60FC90}--\bytes{0x60FC92}. No reader of those cells was found anywhere in
\texttt{prom\_a}, so the remote unit's identity is remembered and, in this firmware,
never used.

\section{Short forms}
\estab\ These sequences terminate within a few bytes. Each is also present verbatim as an
outgoing template in \texttt{prom\_b} between \bytes{0xF4FEB4} and \bytes{0xF4FF60}, which
corroborates the grammar independently.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llc}
\toprule
bytes & meaning & internal command \\
\midrule
\bytes{F0 50 23 7E F7} & acknowledge (ACK) & \bytes{0x01} \\
\bytes{F0 50 24 7E F7} & not acknowledged (NAK) & \bytes{0x02} \\
\bytes{F0 50 27 7E F7} & end of category & \bytes{0x03} \\
\bytes{F0 50 28 7E F7} & end of dump & \bytes{0x04} \\
\bytes{F0 50 29 7E F7} & abort / gave up & \bytes{0x05} \\
\bytes{F0 50 2A 7E F7} & status \bytes{0x16} & \bytes{0x04} \\
\bytes{F0 50 21 04 \textit{nn} 11 F7} & enquiry & \bytes{0x07} \\
\bytes{F0 50 22 04 \textit{nn} 11 F7} & enquiry reply & \bytes{0x08} \\
\bytes{F0 7E 7F 09 01 F7} & GM System On & \bytes{0x20} \\
\bytes{F0 7E 7F 09 02 F7} & GM System Off & \bytes{0x21} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

where \textit{nn} is \bytes{0x00} or \bytes{0x01}.

\estab\ \bytes{0x2A} yields the same internal command number as \bytes{0x28}.

\section{The parameter families}
\estab\ \bytes{0x2B} and \bytes{0x2C} each open into a large parameter-address subtree ---
3756 accepted byte sequences apiece --- and \bytes{0x2D} carries 16, ending in internal
command ids \bytes{0x0B}, \bytes{0x0C}, \bytes{0x0E} and \bytes{0x12}--\bytes{0x16}. A
matched sequence yields a command number bounded by \texttt{cp A,0x22} and used to index
one of two 34-entry handler tables at \bytes{0xF4F800} and \bytes{0xF4F888}.

\likely\ The \bytes{0x2B} and \bytes{0x2C} families are single-parameter traffic rather
than bulk transfer. Their individual parameter addresses are not enumerated in this
edition.
